BATTLELAB — BATTLE RULES
Updated: 27 September 2026

PURPOSE
Record the current battle rules and keep unresolved design choices separate.
This document takes precedence over older proposals for simultaneous move
selection in the README and starter-code comments.

1. TEAM SELECTION
- A match has two players.
- Each player secretly selects three characters from the available roster
  (currently planned to contain approximately ten characters).
- Neither player sees the opponent's selections before the battle starts.
- Both teams are locked in before the battle starts. Players cannot replace
  their selected team members during the match.

2. TEAM REVEAL
- Working rule: reveal all three characters on each team to the opponent
  when the battle starts, after both teams have been locked in.
- This is the current preferred approach, still open to team discussion.
- Reason: secret selection prevents counter-picking during selection, while
  revealing teams at battle start supports informed move and switch decisions.
- Revealing the team does not reveal the opponent's future actions.

3. ACTIVE CHARACTERS
- Each player fields one active character at a time.
- Their other two characters begin in reserve.
- Combat is one active character against one active character, not three
  characters acting against three characters at once.

4. TURNS AND ACTIONS
- Players take alternating turns. This follows the decision that switching
  uses the current player's turn and hands play to the opponent.
- A player may take exactly one action on their turn.
- Actions are resolved on that player's turn; the game does not wait for
  both players to secretly submit an action together.
- A normal turn action is either using a character move or switching to
  another available character on the player's locked-in team.
- The method for deciding who takes the first turn is not yet agreed.

5. CHARACTER MOVES
- Each character has its own attributes and moves reflecting its identity.
- The exact move list and number of moves per character are not yet agreed.
- Generic Fight and Defend buttons are not required. Players can select
  character-specific moves directly, including defensive moves if designed.
- Switching is a shared action available to all characters when the player
  has an eligible replacement.
- Earlier examples such as Shield Bash, Heavy Swing, cooldowns, and conditional
  damage bonuses are ideas only, not approved mechanics.
- Status effects are undecided and are not required for the initial prototype.

6. VOLUNTARY SWITCHING
- On their turn, a player may replace their active character with another
  non-knocked-out character from their own selected team.
- Switching consumes the player's entire turn.
- The player cannot also use a move on that turn.
- After the switch, it is the opponent's turn.

7. KNOCKOUTS AND FREE REPLACEMENT
- A character is knocked out when its health reaches zero and cannot continue
  fighting or be selected as a replacement.
- If the active character is knocked out and the player has characters left,
  that player chooses which eligible reserve character to deploy.
- This forced replacement is free: it does not consume the player's turn.
- Replacing a knocked-out character is different from voluntarily switching.
- After replacement, normal turn order continues; replacement does not grant
  the opponent an additional turn.

8. VICTORY
- A player wins when all three opposing characters have been knocked out.
- If the defeated player has no eligible replacement, the match ends.

9. DECISIONS STILL TO MAKE
- How the first player is chosen.
- How each player chooses their initial active character, and when that
  choice happens relative to the team reveal.
- Whether a team may contain duplicate characters.
- Final confirmation of revealing all three opposing characters at battle start.
- Character attributes, move counts, move effects, and damage values.
- Whether moves use cooldowns, resources, or other limits.
- Whether status effects belong in the game at all.
- Rules for disconnects, inactivity, surrender, and any possible draw.

IMPLEMENTATION NOTE
The older simultaneous-action design included speed and action priority.
Do not carry those mechanics into the alternating-turn version automatically.
Any timing, priority, or speed mechanic needs a clear purpose under these rules.
